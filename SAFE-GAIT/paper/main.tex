\title{SAFE-GAIT: When Shift Warnings Fail in Parkinson's Freezing-of-Gait Monitoring}
\begin{abstract}
Wearable freezing-of-gait (FoG) classifiers may fail when deployment conditions differ from training. We audit Daphnet-trained accelerometer classifiers and exploratory failure warnings on a previously analyzed FoG-STAR subset. A magnitude-only model achieved mean source participant-grouped AUROC 0.816 but target AUROC 0.354. Under an end-of-window, one-second alert-hold convention, it matched zero of 28 binary-label FoG runs and generated six unmatched alert runs. Source-only feature-distance abstention reduced unmatched alerts to five but still missed every episode. A score-drift cutoff flagged six of nine target participants when calibrated on pooled source folds but only one when calibrated on individual held-out source participants. These exploratory results highlight the sensitivity of warnings to calibration units and the danger of equating fewer alerts with reliable detection. The full FoG-STAR v3 dataset requires independent provenance validation and reanalysis. No clinical safety or deployment claim is made.
\end{abstract}
\keywords{Parkinson's disease, freezing of gait, trustworthy AI, abstention, calibration, distribution shift}

\section{Introduction}
Wearable FoG detection and cueing have an established history \cite{bachlin2010}. Cross-dataset degradation has also been reported \cite{lin2026}. We examine whether simple failure-warning mechanisms recognize a documented transfer failure.
\section{Related Work}
Reject-option classification is well established \cite{geifman2019}, as is the need to assess calibration \cite{guo2017}. We therefore do not claim invention of abstention, score drift, or external FoG testing. The contribution is a reproducible, bounded failure audit reporting event-level costs and warning calibration sensitivity.
\section{Data and Provenance}
Daphnet serves as source; back-worn accelerometer measurements from a nine-participant FoG-STAR subset serve as a shifted target. The FoG-STAR publication describes a larger dataset \cite{fogstar2026}, and Zenodo v3 record 17838806 requires full-manifest verification. Historical subset findings must not be confused with full-cohort evaluation. The original FoG-STAR data descriptor reports 22 participants, 329{,}027 sensor rows, and 101 expert-annotated episodes; these published quantities should not be treated as independently verified raw-file audit outputs in our study. The source publication describes video-based adjudication of FoG onset and offset, with multiple manifestations potentially occurring within one episode. Accordingly, our naive contiguous label-run count cannot be substituted for the authors' clinical episode definition without a reconciliation study. We will report distinct denominators for clinically annotated episodes, time-contiguous positive runs, and events eligible under the specified two-second window policy.
\section{Research Questions and Evaluation Design}
We examine three questions on a historical, previously inspected target subset. \textbf{RQ1:} Does improved participant-grouped source discrimination imply target transfer? \textbf{RQ2:} Can source-only feature-distance abstention identify dangerous target failures without withholding clinically relevant alerts? \textbf{RQ3:} How sensitive is a participant-level score-shift warning to its source calibration unit? These questions are exploratory, and the resulting estimates are descriptive. We do not preregister hypotheses retrospectively or present post-hoc choices as prospective selection.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Preprocessing and source-only learning}
Daphnet is the training cohort, with source subjects kept together in five-fold cross-validation. Nonexperiment labels are excluded rather than relabeled as non-FoG. Features are extracted in two-second windows with a one-second stride, subject to explicit annotation-validity and finite-signal rules. For the external-subset replay, target recording boundaries, task changes and timestamp discontinuities separate runs. All model fitting, imputation and gate thresholds use Daphnet, with FoG-STAR labels reserved for retrospective scoring. The classifier is a histogram gradient-boosting model with fixed threshold 0.5; a 12-feature axis-dependent representation is contrasted with three orientation-invariant magnitude statistics. These are deliberately simple stress-test baselines, not claimed state-of-the-art detectors.

\subsection{Abstention, coverage, and warning calibration}
A robust source-based distance gate rejects windows that exceed a threshold selected for a stated source-retention fraction. We report target acceptance separately from source retention. In a system with no human fallback, an abstained prediction must not count as a correct negative or a successful FoG alarm. We therefore count every reference event missed by an abstaining policy in the overall missed-event total.

For score-shift warnings, a supremum empirical-CDF distance compares predicted-score distributions. The first pilot calibrated its percentile cutoff across pooled held-out source-fold scores; the revised pilot calibrates on individual held-out source subjects, matching the intended patient-level target unit more closely. Neither threshold provides a prospective false-warning guarantee: there are few source participants, dependence remains, and the post-hoc design does not establish exchangeability.

\subsection{Event-level decision policy}
Every prediction is issued at its window end. Historical replay represents an emitted positive prediction as a one-second alert interval. Raw contiguous positive-label runs are reference events under the stated implementation, and one-to-one matching uses interval overlap without temporal tolerance or alert merging. The numerator for event sensitivity is the number of matched reference runs; the denominator includes all reference runs, including short events not successfully covered by decisions. False-alert burden is unmatched alert runs divided by annotated recording hours. A protocol-sensitivity study will vary the alert hold duration and matching tolerance before any stronger event-level inference.

\subsection{Inference and reporting}
Because adjacent windows overlap and observations are clustered by participant, independent-window standard errors are inappropriate. Descriptive bootstrap resampling is performed at participant level; uncertainty estimates are interpreted cautiously with only nine target participants. We separate source fold means, target discrimination, target fixed-threshold event sensitivity, abstention coverage and warning counts rather than combining them into a single safety score.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Source discrimination is not deployment reliability}
The higher source AUROC of the magnitude representation did not translate into useful target detection at the fixed operating threshold. This result is consistent with the possibility that sensor placement and task context alter the relationship between measured acceleration and FoG labels. The experiment cannot isolate which factor caused the failure. It also cannot establish that alternative sensor placements, algorithms, or recalibrated policies would fail.

\subsection{An abstention policy can conceal missed events}
A reduction from six to five unmatched alert runs is not a safety improvement when none of the 28 reference runs is detected. Selective performance must be coupled with the fraction of clinically relevant episodes withheld by abstention and the consequences of a missing alert. In particular, apparently high target coverage does not imply coverage of the hard, positive cases.

\subsection{Calibration unit is part of the estimand}
The shift-warning pilot changes markedly when calibration switches from pooled source folds to held-out individuals. The two warning fractions cannot be interpreted as competing calibrated detection rates because the underlying null reference units differ. The result is a methodological warning about matching the reference unit to the actual decision unit, not a proof that one procedure possesses finite-sample error control.

\subsection{Evidence required to strengthen the claim}
The full FoG-STAR v3 sensor and clinical files need exact file-level verification, and its expert episode conventions must be reconciled with our binary-label-run implementation. Any subsequent full-v3 result is also exploratory because target data influenced prior analyses. Stronger comparative evidence requires frequency-domain FoG baselines, prospective or genuinely untouched external evaluation, explicit alert-timing sensitivity, subject-level uncertainty and a clinically defensible intervention policy.

\section{Reproducibility and Traceability}
The provenance gate requires a separately verified dataset manifest and refuses to launch experiments unless all mandatory checks pass. The run orchestrator records SHA-256 hashes for source and target files, the manifest, each output, executed commands, software environment and per-step failure status; it also refuses stale results filenames. The episode-reconciliation script records participant/session/task discontinuities and requires explicit timestamp units. These controls improve reproducibility but do not guarantee clinical annotation validity.

Historical AUROC estimates come from cross-dataset window-classification reports; zero-of-28 FoG run matching comes from the older event replay under zero matching tolerance and an assumed one-second alert hold. The warning flags come from a separate ECDF-score diagnostic. The complete v3 data and source-paper expert event conventions remain to be reconciled. New full-v3 results will remain exploratory because the target has previously informed analysis decisions.

\section{Limitations and Ethics}
The FoG-STAR subset contains only nine sensor participant IDs and was previously inspected. Target sensor position, activity context and annotation differ from Daphnet. Calibration statistics have no formal finite-sample guarantee, and event matching uses a zero-tolerance convention. This study is neither prospective nor clinically validated. Results should not be used to automate care.
\section{Event-Timing Protocol Sensitivity}
The existing historical replay models an emitted positive decision as an alert interval lasting one second. That fixed hold duration is an assumption and not a measured clinical alarm persistence. We therefore provide a separately testable protocol-sensitivity analysis varying hypothetical alert hold duration (instantaneous, 0.25 s and 1 s) and event-matching tolerance (0, 0.5 and 1 s). This design preserves decision causality by assigning every decision to the completion of its feature window, and it forbids retrospective attribution of predictions to an earlier time. Results for the full-v3 release are pending. Consequently, the exploratory 0/28 count must be interpreted under the specific historical replay assumptions and not as a clinically validated zero-detection estimate.

\section{Pre-submission Validation Gates}
The complete v3 manifest and expert episode conventions, subject-independent reruns, comparison against strong frequency-based and external-validation baselines, subject-clustered uncertainty, and final citation metadata must be checked before submission. These gates are documented in \texttt{REVIEWER\_READINESS\_GATE.md}. The historical nine-participant subset is not an untouched external test cohort and must not be passed off as the full published dataset.

\section{Conclusion}
Simple out-of-distribution warning and abstention mechanisms did not rescue a failed transfer model. Calibration units, event sensitivity, and false-alert burden must be reported jointly before reliability claims.
